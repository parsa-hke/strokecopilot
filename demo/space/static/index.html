Can a language model recommend stroke treatment and show its working?

Two vision models read a CT scan. A language model reads their numbers and a triage note, then decides on clot-busting drugs and clot removal against a rulebook written from the 2026 guideline. A verifier then checks every claim it makes against the scan's numbers, the note and the rulebook.

        About this project

        What it is not

        A research prototype built for a university course. It is not a medical device, has not been validated clinically, and must never be used to decide anyone's treatment. The rulebook is the team's reading of the guideline and has not been reviewed by a clinician.

        Limits we report

        • The "core" is a model trained on final infarct, not acute core, in a cohort that was reperfused.
        • Real cases have no ASPECTS and mostly an unknown occlusion site, so the rules leave many decisions undetermined.
        • The bleed detector flags about half of ISLES'24's presumed bleed-free scans: a domain shift, not an RSNA failure.
        • Hallucination rates are the verifier's; humans confirmed about a third of its flags.
