01 · Overview

What was built

A pipeline of five parts. Each one is tested on its own, then all together on patients none of it had seen.

  1. Core and penumbraAn nnU-Net, trained five times on different folds of ISLES'24, outlines the tissue already lost. Tissue at risk is the guideline's own rule: Tmax over 6 seconds.
  2. Bleed checkA ResNet-50 trained on RSNA scans reads the plain CT for any bleed, which rules out the clot-busting drug.
  3. The rulebookThe 2026 guideline rewritten in the team's own words as 47 passages, and a program that applies them to a case.
  4. The reasonerA language model reads the scan's numbers, a triage note and the whole rulebook, and decides on each treatment with cited reasons.
  5. The verifierEvery answer is split into claims, and each claim is traced to the scan, the note or a passage. What does not trace is a hallucination.

    07 · Limits and credit

    Limits and credit

    What it is not

    A research prototype built for a university course. It is not a medical device, has not been validated clinically, and must never be used to decide anyone's treatment. The rulebook is the team's reading of the guideline and has not been reviewed by a clinician.

    Limits we report

    • The "core" is a model trained on final infarct, not acute core, in a cohort that was reperfused.
    • Real cases have no ASPECTS and mostly an unknown occlusion site, so the rules leave many decisions undetermined.
    • The bleed detector flags about half of ISLES'24's presumed bleed-free scans: a domain shift, not an RSNA failure.
    • Hallucination rates are the verifier's; humans confirmed about a third of its flags.
